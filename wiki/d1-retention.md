# D1 Retention

**Summary**: The percentage of players who return to a game on the day after first install. Used as the leading indicator of whether a game's core mechanic is working.

**Sources**: `Making a Hit Idle Game – Eight Lessons from Kolibri Games.md`.

**Last updated**: 2026-04-25.

---

## Benchmarks (mobile)

- **~40%** — typical mobile baseline cited by [[kolibri-games]] (source: Making a Hit Idle Game – Eight Lessons from Kolibri Games.md). Genre-agnostic in the source — needs verification across other references.
- **63–81%** — what the [[idle-miner-tycoon]] MVP achieved.

## Why focus on D1 specifically

Per Nate Barker: "By focusing on D1 retention, we gave ourselves more wiggle room for the later aspects of the game. If we got something wrong further down the line, we could fix it without affecting our D1 figures."

D1 measures whether the [[core-gameplay-loop]] is compelling enough to bring a player back once. If that signal is strong, downstream issues (monetization tuning, late-game content, social features) are recoverable. If D1 is weak, no amount of polish on later systems will save the game — which is why it functions as a go/no-go signal during [[mvp-prototyping]].
