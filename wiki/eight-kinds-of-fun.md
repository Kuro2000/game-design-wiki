# Eight Kinds of Fun

**Summary**: The aesthetic taxonomy from the [[mda-framework]] paper — eight different things players mean when they say "fun." Used to replace the vague word "fun" with vocabulary precise enough to design against.

**Sources**: `MDA Framework - Hunicke LeBlanc Zubek.md`; `MDA & 8 Kinds of Fun.md`.

**Last updated**: 2026-04-25.

---

## The eight aesthetics

| # | Aesthetic | Game as... |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Sensation** | Sense-pleasure |
| 2 | **Fantasy** | Make-believe |
| 3 | **Narrative** | Drama |
| 4 | **Challenge** | Obstacle course |
| 5 | **Fellowship** | Social framework |
| 6 | **Discovery** | Uncharted territory |
| 7 | **Expression** | Self-discovery |
| 8 | **Submission** | Pastime |

(Source: `MDA Framework - Hunicke LeBlanc Zubek.md`, p. 3.)

The original paper is explicit that the list "is by no means complete or final" — it's a working vocabulary, not a closed taxonomy.

## What each aesthetic actually means

- **Sensation** — visceral pleasure: visuals, audio, controller feedback. Why a beautiful or punchy game can hook even when the systems are simple.
- **Fantasy** — the player gets to be someone else, somewhere else. Settings and personas matter even more than mechanics.
- **Narrative** — drama: plot, character, dilemmas. The game tells a story or generates one through play.
- **Challenge** — overcoming obstacles via skill. The classic "fun" most non-designers default to.
- **Fellowship** — playing *with* people. Cooperation, communication, shared identity.
- **Discovery** — uncovering things hidden. New areas, secret mechanics, surprising emergent behaviour.
- **Expression** — making something that feels like *yours*. Character creation, base-building, creative play.
- **Submission** — comfortable repetition. Cozy idle play, daily logins, low-stakes grinding. Not pejorative — submission is a real and valuable aesthetic.

## How games combine aesthetics

Hunicke et al. give example mixes (source: MDA Framework paper):

| Game | Aesthetic profile |
|---|---|
| Charades | Fellowship, Expression, Challenge |
| Quake | Challenge, Sensation, Competition, Fantasy |
| The Sims | Discovery, Fantasy, Expression, Narrative |
| Final Fantasy | Fantasy, Narrative, Expression, Discovery, Challenge, Submission |

Most successful games hit 3–5 aesthetics. A game with only one becomes thin. A game with all eight diluted is incoherent.

## Worked example: BotW

Jenny Wang's [[mda-and-8-kinds-of-fun-jenny-wang|article]] decomposes *Breath of the Wild* into Narrative + Fantasy + Challenge + Discovery + Expression + Sensation — six aesthetics, with Fellowship and Submission notably absent. That absence is informative: BotW isn't a co-op or daily-grind game.

## Why a designer should care

- **Target then build.** Pick the 3–5 aesthetics you're going for and check every design decision against them.
- **Diagnose what's missing.** A vague "this game isn't quite fun" almost always resolves into "we said Challenge but we accidentally built Submission."
- **Communicate intent.** Saying "we want Fellowship + Discovery" is much more actionable across a team than "we want fun."
