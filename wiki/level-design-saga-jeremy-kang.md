# Level Design Saga: Creating Levels for Casual Games

**Summary**: Jeremy Kang (Principal Game Designer, [[king-games]] Berlin) walks through how King designs levels for [[saga-games|Saga]] titles — the four-principle framework (Hooks, Difficulty, Flow, Rhythm), the five-stage level pipeline, and the testing/balancing process.

**Sources**: `Level Design Saga - Jeremy Kang.md` (extracted from `KANG_Jeremy_LevelDesignSaga.pdf`, GDC 2016).

**Last updated**: 2026-04-25.

---

## Why the talk is narrow on purpose

Kang frames the talk as *level design × casual × Saga* — a deliberately narrow slice. Most published level-design material is about core/console games; this talk fills the casual mobile gap (source: Level Design Saga - Jeremy Kang.md).

## The four principles

Adapted from Ed Byrne's *Game Level Design*:

| Principle | Job |
|---|---|
| [[level-hooks\|Hooks]] | Make levels feel different from each other. |
| [[level-difficulty\|Difficulty]] | Keep the player challenged. |
| [[level-flow\|Flow]] | Keep the player moving toward the goal. |
| [[level-rhythm\|Rhythm]] | Vary the player's emotional experience. |

## The five-stage pipeline

[[level-design-process]] → Concept → Layout → Creation → Balancing → Testing.

![The five-stage level design process.](assets/level-design-saga-jeremy-kang/page-22.png)

Within Creation specifically: 1) lay out tiles, 2) set objectives, 3) place game objects, 4) place blockers, 5) test.

## Tools and frameworks Kang draws on

- **[[mda-framework]]** — Mechanics → Dynamics → Aesthetics. Level design is where mechanics become dynamics.
- **[[ki-sho-ten-ketsu]]** — 4-part Japanese narrative structure used to pace level sequences (introduction → development → twist → conclusion).
- **[[flow-theory]]** — Csíkszentmihályi (via Jenova Chen) for keeping difficulty in the engagement zone.
- **Beat charts and level libraries** — production artefacts for tracking pacing across hundreds of levels (see [[level-rhythm]]).

## Testing & KPIs

[[level-testing]] is staged: self-test → internal team test → qualitative user test → playtest releases. KPIs the team watches: difficulty, retention, progression, monetisation.

## Memorable framings

- "Level design is where the magician meets the mathematician."
- The level designer is "the invisible hand that guides players forward — the more aware the player is of this, the less fun it'll be."
- Candy Crush's *Level 65* as a case study: a hated level rebalanced into a beloved one.

## The Saga envelope

[[saga-games]] = linear progression, 100s–1000s of levels, new content every 1–2 weeks, light social, casual audience. The format demands volume, which is why the pipeline and tools matter so much (source: Level Design Saga - Jeremy Kang.md).
