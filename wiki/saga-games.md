# Saga Games

**Summary**: King's term for a particular casual-game format: linear progression through hundreds-to-thousands of bite-size levels, with new levels released every 1–2 weeks and light social features. The format constrains a lot about how levels must be designed.

**Sources**: `Level Design Saga - Jeremy Kang.md`.

**Last updated**: 2026-04-25.

---

## The "Saga envelope"

Per Jeremy Kang at [[king-games]] (source: Level Design Saga - Jeremy Kang.md):

- **Linear progression** — players move through levels in order.
- **100s–1000s of levels** — depth as longevity.
- **New levels every 1–2 weeks** — see [[live-ops-features]] for the cadence pattern.
- **Light social elements** — leaderboards, asks for lives, etc.
- **Casual audience** — short sessions, low barrier.

## Implications for design

- **Volume forces tooling.** The [[level-design-process]] and [[blocker-framework]] aren't aesthetic preferences — they're survival mechanics for a thousand-level pipeline.
- **Pacing matters across hundreds of hours.** [[level-rhythm]] becomes a meta-design problem, not just an in-level one. Tools: beat charts, level libraries, [[ki-sho-ten-ketsu]] applied across level groups.
- **Failure is cheap and frequent.** Players replay; the design assumes that. Difficulty is tuned around cumulative attempts (see [[level-testing]]).
- **Retention is a leading metric.** [[d1-retention]] still matters, but Saga games live or die on long-tail retention curves measured in months.

## Where Saga-format games show up

- The [[candy-crush-franchise]] (Candy Crush Saga, Soda, Jelly, Friends).
- Bubble Witch Saga, Farm Heroes Saga, Pet Rescue Saga (other King titles).
- The format has been copied widely outside King.
