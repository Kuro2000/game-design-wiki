# Idle Games

**Summary**: A genre (also called "clicker" games) where the central activity is incrementing a currency that, when spent, accelerates further increments. Often described as "a spreadsheet with an interface."

**Sources**: `Making a Hit Idle Game – Eight Lessons from Kolibri Games.md`.

**Last updated**: 2026-04-25.

---

## Definition

Per Nate Barker of [[kolibri-games]], "an idle game is a spreadsheet with an interface" — accurate if unflattering. Players start with a manually-incremented score (Cookie Clicker's cookie tap), then trade currency for upgrades that automate or accelerate the increment (source: Making a Hit Idle Game – Eight Lessons from Kolibri Games.md).

## Common shapes

The genre clusters around two recognisable templates:

- **Business sims** (factories, restaurants, mines).
- **Tower-builders** (vertical stacking of floors/levels).

[[idle-miner-tycoon]] differentiated itself with a small inversion: a tower-builder that dug *down* instead of building up.

## Defining properties

- **You can't lose.** Progression speed, not survival, is the variable players care about — which is why [[pay-or-wait-monetization]] works so cleanly.
- **The [[core-gameplay-loop]] is short and visible**, often shown on one screen.
- **Progression is gated by [[choke-points]]** — explicit bottlenecks players upgrade past.
- **Retention is the leading indicator of fit.** A working idle prototype shows D1 well above genre baseline; see [[d1-retention]].
