# Player Motivation Models

**Summary**: Cross-comparison of three frameworks the wiki uses to talk about what players want from games — Bartle's player types, MDA's 8 kinds of fun, and Quantic Foundry's empirical Gamer Motivation Model. Each is useful for a different purpose.

**Sources**: `Bartle's Player Types for Gamification.md`; `MDA Framework - Hunicke LeBlanc Zubek.md`; `Gamer Motivation Model - Quantic Foundry.md`.

**Last updated**: 2026-04-25.

---

## The three frames at a glance

| | [[bartle-player-types\|Bartle]] | [[eight-kinds-of-fun\|MDA Aesthetics]] | [[gamer-motivation-model\|Quantic Foundry]] |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Year** | 1996 | 2004 | ~2015 onward |
| **Origin** | Observation of MUD players | Theoretical taxonomy | Factor analysis on 1.25M-player survey |
| **Output** | 4 categorical types | 8 aesthetic categories | 12 continuous motivations / 6 clusters |
| **Best for** | Quick segmentation talk | Designer intent vocabulary | Audience targeting + data-driven design |
| **Caveat** | Population fractions are MUD-era guesses | List is "not exhaustive or terminal" | Survey-based; depends on self-report |

## Where the three overlap

| Concept | Bartle | MDA Aesthetic | Quantic Foundry |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mastering hard things | Achiever (partly) | Challenge | Challenge |
| Beating others | Killer | (implied in Challenge) | Competition |
| Co-op / friendship | Socializer | Fellowship | Community |
| Probing the unknown | Explorer | Discovery | Discovery |
| Be someone else, elsewhere | — | Fantasy | Fantasy |
| Drama, plot | — | Narrative | Story |
| Visceral pleasure | — | Sensation | Excitement (partial) |
| Self-expression / creation | — | Expression | Design |
| Cozy repetition / grinding | — | Submission | (Completion + Power partial) |
| Visible status / progress | Achiever | (implied) | Power, Completion |

A few takeaways:

- **Bartle is the simplest frame** — and it shows. Three of MDA's eight aesthetics (Fantasy, Narrative, Sensation) have no Bartle analogue. Several Quantic Foundry motivations (Story, Design, Strategy) don't reduce cleanly to Bartle's categories either.
- **MDA's "Submission" is unusual.** It's the most distinctive of MDA's eight — neither Bartle nor Quantic Foundry really capture "comfortable repetition" as a positive motivation. Important for understanding [[idle-games]] and casual mobile play.
- **Quantic Foundry adds Power and Completion** as separate motivations, where Bartle would lump both into "Achiever."

## When to use which

- **Bartle** when you want a fast shared-language sketch ("our audience is mostly Socializers"). Easy to explain in a meeting; don't bet money on the percentages.
- **MDA Aesthetics** when you're authoring a game and want to crystallise the *experience you're targeting*. Best as a designer-side vocabulary: "we want Discovery + Expression + Submission."
- **Quantic Foundry** when you want *data-grounded audience targeting*: "our analytics say players cluster on Strategy + Completion, so let's invest in long-term progression and avoid heavy real-time competition." Also best for cross-segment comparison.

The three are not in competition — they're three lenses for the same underlying question: "what does this player want from the next 30 minutes of play?"

## Caveats common to all three

- **Players are mixtures.** Even in the categorical models (Bartle), people have dominant + secondary types. Treat any single-label characterisation as an approximation.
- **Context shifts motivation.** The same person plays Stardew Valley one evening and Counter-Strike the next — Quantic Foundry data captures this, Bartle and MDA don't really account for it.
- **Cultural/demographic differences exist.** Quantic Foundry's data shows real differences across age, gender, and region; Bartle's MUD-era ratios certainly don't generalise.
