# Gamer Motivation Model

**Summary**: Quantic Foundry's empirical model of gamer motivations — 12 motivations grouped into 6 clusters, distilled from factor analysis on 1.25M+ player survey responses. Each motivation is a spectrum, with both ends carrying meaningful preference.

**Sources**: `Gamer Motivation Model - Quantic Foundry.md`.

**Last updated**: 2026-04-25.

---

## The structure

![The 12 gamer motivations grouped into 6 clusters.](assets/gamer-motivation-model-quantic-foundry/page-03.png)

| Cluster | Tagline | Pair |
|---|---|---|
| Action | "Boom!" | **Destruction** + **Excitement** |
| Social | "Let's Play Together" | **Competition** + **Community** |
| Mastery | "Let Me Think" | **Challenge** + **Strategy** |
| Achievement | "I Want More" | **Completion** + **Power** |
| Immersion | "Once Upon a Time" | **Fantasy** + **Story** |
| Creativity | "What If?" | **Discovery** + **Design** |

## The three meta-clusters

![The three meta-clusters — Action+Social = BRIGHT, Mastery+Achievement = TALL, Immersion+Creativity = WIDE.](assets/gamer-motivation-model-quantic-foundry/page-04.png)

- **BRIGHT** = Action + Social. Immediate, exciting, adrenaline-driven.
- **TALL** = Mastery + Achievement. Cool-headed, long-term, cumulative.
- **WIDE** = Immersion + Creativity. Expansive, expressive, curious.

## The 12 motivations defined

| Motivation | What players want | Game examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Destruction** | Guns, explosives, mayhem. Chaos. | Call of Duty, Battlefield, Halo. |
| **Excitement** | Fast-paced, thrills, surprises. | CS:GO, Super Smash Bros, BIT.TRIP RUNNER. |
| **Competition** | Duels, matches, rankings. | League of Legends, DotA 2, CS:GO. |
| **Community** | Group play, chat, teamwork. | Final Fantasy XIV, Destiny, Rainbow Six Siege. |
| **Challenge** | Skill, persistence, hard difficulty. | Dark Souls, osu!, Street Fighter V. |
| **Strategy** | Planning, decisions, long-term play. | Civilization, StarCraft II, Crusader Kings II. |
| **Completion** | Finish everything; collectibles, achievements. | Final Fantasy, Lego Dimensions. |
| **Power** | Become powerful in-world. Gear, stats. | World of Warcraft, Diablo III, Summoners War. |
| **Fantasy** | Be someone else, somewhere else. | Skyrim, Mass Effect, Star Wars: KOTOR. |
| **Story** | Elaborate plots, multidimensional characters. | Life is Strange, Persona 4, BioShock. |
| **Discovery** | Tinker, experiment, "what if?" | Elder Scrolls, Minecraft, Legend of Zelda. |
| **Design** | Express individuality through customisation. | Mass Effect, Cities: Skylines, Animal Crossing. |

(Source: Gamer Motivation Model - Quantic Foundry.md.)

## Each motivation is a spectrum

The model is unusual in that the *low* end of each motivation is a real preference, not just absence:

| High motivation | Low end (also a preference) | Low-end games |
|---|---|---|
| Community | Independence | Lego Harry Potter, Farm Heroes Saga |
| Competition | Non-Adversarial | Myst, Gone Home, Dragon Age II |
| Excitement | Calm | Civilization, Master of Orion II |
| Destruction | Enduring | Animal Crossing, Harvest Moon |
| Completion | Self-Driven | RimWorld, Kerbal Space Program |
| Power | Flat-Progression | Night in the Woods, Ico, 80 Days |
| Strategy | Spontaneous | The Sims, Mario Kart Wii |
| Challenge | Easy Fun | Stardew Valley, Animal Crossing |
| Fantasy | Generic/Abstract | Counter-Strike, Candy Crush, World of Tanks |
| Story | Open-Ended | Factorio, SimCity, Quake III Arena |
| Discovery | Practical (fully exposed rules) | FIFA, Mahjong, Scrabble |
| Design | Curated | Braid, Spelunky |

This is what makes the model more design-actionable than typology approaches: a player isn't an "Explorer" or "not an Explorer" — they sit on a continuous Discovery↔Practical axis.

## How it's used

Quantic Foundry sells **Audience Reports** that plot a target audience's motivations as percentile ranks against the full 1.25M-gamer dataset. A Power score of 35% means: this audience prefers Power more than 35% of all gamers. The 50% line is the average. Both highs and lows matter.

![Percentile ranks explained.](assets/gamer-motivation-model-quantic-foundry/page-05.png)

## How it differs from other player models

- vs. **[[bartle-player-types|Bartle]]**: Bartle has 4 categorical types from MUD observation; Quantic Foundry has 12 continuous spectra from factor analysis on a million players. The two overlap (e.g., Bartle's Killer ≈ high Destruction + high Competition) but the empirical model is finer-grained.
- vs. **[[eight-kinds-of-fun|MDA's 8 Aesthetics]]**: The MDA list is theoretical (what designers might aim for); Quantic Foundry's clusters are statistical (what factor-analysis surfaces in player self-reports). Several map cleanly (Story↔Narrative, Community↔Fellowship, Discovery↔Discovery).
