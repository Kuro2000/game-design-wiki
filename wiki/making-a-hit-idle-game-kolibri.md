# Making a Hit Idle Game – Eight Lessons from Kolibri Games

**Summary**: Nate Barker (Director of Business Development at [[kolibri-games]]) walks through how the studio grew from four people in a Frankfurt apartment to a 100+ person company with 100M+ downloads, distilling the experience into eight practical lessons on idle game design, monetization, and live ops.

**Sources**: `Making a Hit Idle Game – Eight Lessons from Kolibri Games.md` (originally published on gameanalytics.com, 2019-10-29).

**Last updated**: 2026-04-25.

---

## The eight lessons

1. **Start with an idea that'll stand out.** [[idle-games]] gravitate toward business sims and tower-builders; Kolibri inverted the tower into a mine and that single twist was enough to differentiate [[idle-miner-tycoon]].
2. **Create a dead simple prototype.** Their motto: "say no to everything you want to do." See [[mvp-prototyping]].
3. **Don't push on with something that's not working.** Their first project, Front Yard Wars, was scrapped at ~60% completion. They blame waterfall, technical complexity, and the absence of testing.
4. **Show the core gameplay loop on one screen.** See [[core-gameplay-loop]] and [[choke-points]].
5. **Base monetization on "pay or wait".** See [[pay-or-wait-monetization]] and [[rewarded-ads]].
6. **Use a lean development process.** See [[lean-development]].
7. **Keep players engaged with frequent updates and live-ops content.** See [[live-ops-features]] and [[staged-rollout]].
8. **Listen to your players** — data plus community feedback shaped what got built.

## Numbers worth remembering

- MVP D1 retention: **63–81%** (vs. ~40% mobile baseline). See [[d1-retention]].
- Avg session length: **~10 minutes**.
- Avg rewarded-ad watches per player per day: **8**.
- Ad engagement rate: **52%**.
- 160+ updates since 2016 launch; cadence ~1/week.

## Contradictions / things to verify

- The article claims "about 60% of our revenue comes from ads" *and* "the other 60% of our revenue comes from in-app purchases" — sums to 120%. One of the figures is likely a typo (the second clause probably should read "rest" or "40%"). Marked for verification (source: Making a Hit Idle Game – Eight Lessons from Kolibri Games.md).
- The "~40% D1 retention as mobile baseline" claim is genre-agnostic in the article. Worth cross-checking against other sources.
