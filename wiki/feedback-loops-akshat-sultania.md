# Feedback Loops in Games (Akshat Sultania)

**Summary**: Akshat Sultania's Medium article (2023) covering positive and negative feedback loops with examples from Call of Duty, Mario Kart, Civilization, XCOM, and others — plus how designers *dampen* runaway positive loops.

**Sources**: `Feedback Loops in Games.md` (Medium, 2023-08-17).

**Last updated**: 2026-04-26.

---

## What the article covers

A pragmatic survey of [[feedback-loops]] in game design. Distinguishes positive (success → more success) from negative (success → harder challenge), and emphasises a third concept the other source skips: **dampening** — taming a positive loop that's grown too strong.

## Examples it uses

| Game | Loop type | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| Call of Duty (Modern Warfare era) | Positive | Killstreaks → airstrikes → more kills → bigger streaks. The rich get richer. |
| Chess | Positive (negative for the loser) | Losing a key piece compresses your future options. |
| Mario Kart | Negative | Last-place gets bullet bills/blue shells; first-place gets bananas. |
| Splatoon | Positive | Inking more ground unlocks fast travel + hidden spots. |
| Team Fortress 2 | Positive | Capturing points reduces your respawn time. |
| Civilization | Mixed | Conquest snowballs *but* unhappiness + empire-management cost push back. |
| Devil May Cry | Positive (problematic) | High level scores grant orbs that make weapons better — gives strong players more help. |
| RPGs (general) | Both | XP curve = positive; level-up cost rising = negative dampener. |

## The XCOM cautionary tale

Sultania flags XCOM as a case where feedback loops break in single-player. Standard JRPG: get XP → become stronger → kill faster → more XP. Easy to escape via restart. **XCOM**: with permadeath + no grinding, when you bite the dust, fallen comrades stay fallen. Experienced troops become juggernauts; new recruits can't catch up. "Not always the recipe for a good time."

(Source: Feedback Loops in Games.md.)

## On dampening

Two concrete mechanisms the article highlights:

1. **Killstreak rebalancing in Call of Duty: Black Ops** — killstreak bonuses no longer count toward future killstreaks. Players have to actually kill with their own gun to keep escalating.
2. **Mario Kart 8 Super Horn** — counter-item to the blue shell. Adds an answer rather than removing the negative loop.

## Why hide a feedback loop?

Sultania notes Resident Evil 4's hidden [[dynamic-difficulty]] system — a negative loop that's specifically *not* surfaced to players, because telling skilled players "the game gets harder if you play well" is effectively telling them to underperform.
