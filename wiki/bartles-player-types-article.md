# Bartle's Player Types for Gamification (Kumar, Herger, Dam)

**Summary**: An Interaction Design Foundation article by Janaki Kumar, Mario Herger, and Rikke Friis Dam (2017) summarising Richard Bartle's classic four player-type taxonomy and how to apply it in gamification design.

**Sources**: `Bartle's Player Types for Gamification.md` (ixdf.org, 2017).

**Last updated**: 2026-04-25.

---

## What the article covers

A pragmatic introduction to [[bartle-player-types]] — Achievers, Explorers, Socializers, Killers — written for gamification designers (not pure game designers). The framing is: "the better you understand your players, the better you can cater to their needs."

## The four types and the (claimed) population split

Per Bartle as cited in the article (source: Bartle's Player Types for Gamification.md):

| Type | What they want | Approx. share |
|---|---|---|
| **Achiever** | Points, badges, status. Visible progress. | ~10% |
| **Explorer** | Discovery, secrets, Easter eggs. | ~10% |
| **Socializer** | Connection, collaboration. | ~80% |
| **Killer** | Beating others. Direct competition. | <1% |

⚠️ The "80% Socializer / <1% Killer" figures should be treated cautiously — they predate widespread free-to-play / esports gaming and the article itself doesn't cite the underlying data. See [[player-motivation-models]] for cross-comparison with Quantic Foundry's data, which paints a more even distribution. **Verify before quoting.**

## Practical advice the article emphasises

- **Don't stereotype.** Most players display some traits in more than one category — but most have a *dominant* trait.
- **Survey/observe rather than guess.** The article links to the Bartle Test as a starting point.
- **Design for the dominant + accommodate the others.** "Use it to inform the majority of features you will use, and then just add a small number of features that appeal to each of the other categories."

## Quote worth remembering

> "Stereotypes are fast and easy, but they are lies and the truth takes its time."
> — Deb Caletti (cited in the article)

## Caveats this article surfaces

- The article was written for gamification (workplace software, learning systems), not just games. The advice generalises but the player population may differ.
- Bartle's original taxonomy comes from MUDs — text-based multiplayer worlds. Whether his population fractions hold in modern mobile / casual / single-player gaming is unclear.
