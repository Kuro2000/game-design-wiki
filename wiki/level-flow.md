# Level Flow

**Summary**: The principle of keeping players moving toward the goal. The level designer is "the invisible hand" — players shouldn't notice they're being guided. *Distinct from* [[flow-theory]] (Csíkszentmihályi), though related.

**Sources**: `Level Design Saga - Jeremy Kang.md`.

**Last updated**: 2026-04-25.

---

## How to guide without being obvious

Per Jeremy Kang (source: Level Design Saga - Jeremy Kang.md):

- **Signposting** — visual cues toward what to do next.
- **Goals and sub-goals** — explicit and implicit micro-targets.
- **Hints** — proactive suggestions when the player gets stuck.
- The level designer is "the invisible hand that guides players forward. The more aware the player is of this, the less fun it'll be."

## Flow vs. [[flow-theory]]

Two different concepts that share a name:

| | Level flow (this page) | Flow theory |
|---|---|---|
| What it is | Guidance through a level toward the goal | A psychological state of optimal engagement |
| Who | Ed Byrne / Jeremy Kang | Mihály Csíkszentmihályi |
| Used for | Level layout decisions | Difficulty calibration |

Both apply to level design — Kang invokes both — but they're not the same thing.
