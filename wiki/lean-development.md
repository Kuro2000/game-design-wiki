# Lean Development

**Summary**: Game production approach borrowed from Eric Ries' *The Lean Startup*: never do more than is needed to reach the next decision point about whether the game is worth continuing.

**Sources**: `Making a Hit Idle Game – Eight Lessons from Kolibri Games.md`.

**Last updated**: 2026-04-25.

---

## Origin

[[kolibri-games]] cite *The Lean Startup* by Eric Ries as the main inspiration (source: Making a Hit Idle Game – Eight Lessons from Kolibri Games.md). The core import: "Games are a hit-driven business. The faster you can figure out that a game isn't working, the lower the costs."

## The five-step Kolibri pipeline

Refined for the development of [[idle-factory-tycoon]]:

1. **Small team** finds a concept and core mechanic.
2. **Simple prototype** built, tested, refined — see [[mvp-prototyping]].
3. **Internal testing** — every team member plays it. Decision point at ~8 weeks.
4. **External testing** for ~4 weeks — track early-day retention (especially [[d1-retention]]), organic downloads, ratings, qualitative feedback.
5. **Scale up** — grow team, build features, scale to user numbers.

## What it cost them not to do this

Front Yard Wars — Kolibri's pre-Idle-Miner-Tycoon project — was killed at ~60% completion. Their post-mortem named three failure modes (source: Making a Hit Idle Game – Eight Lessons from Kolibri Games.md):

- Waterfall instead of agile development.
- Overly complex technical pipeline.
- No testing for months.

Lean development is, in part, the institutionalised response to that experience.
