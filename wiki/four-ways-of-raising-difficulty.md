# Four Ways of Raising Difficulty (Match-3)

**Summary**: Lucien Chen's enumeration of the four levers a level designer can pull to make a [[match-3-games|match-3]] level harder. They compose: any level uses some combination of the four.

**Sources**: `GDC2020 Blockers - Lucien Chen.md`.

**Last updated**: 2026-04-25.

---

## The four levers

1. **[[blockers|Blockers]]** — what obstacles are on the board and how many. The most-discussed lever in Chen's talk; classified via the [[blocker-framework]].
2. **Spawn rate** — what tiles appear and how often. Sub-levers:
    - Rate of special candies / blockers / regular candies.
    - Frequency of objective spawning.
    - Number of candy colours (more colours = harder, since matches are rarer).
3. **[[match-3-design-styles|Design styles]]** — five player-experience archetypes (Explosive, Snipey, Journey, Puzzly, Grindy) that dictate the *feel* of a level.
4. **Level layout** — board shape and constraints:
    - Open vs. narrow board.
    - Empty positions / holes.
    - Multi-screen levels.

(Source: GDC2020 Blockers - Lucien Chen.md, pp. 4–8.)

## Why it's useful as a checklist

Most level designers reach for *blockers* first when a level is "too easy." Chen's framing reminds them the other three levers exist — sometimes adding a colour, narrowing the board, or shifting the design style does what an extra blocker can't, with less player frustration.
